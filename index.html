              Method and limitations
              
    
                
      
              • United Kingdom only. Every request uses Google Trends geo GB (England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland).
                • 
      
              • Relative interest, not volume. Each value is a term's share of all UK Google searches, scaled 0–100 within one request. A falling index can sit alongside flat absolute searches when total search grows.
                • 
      
              • Week, month and year-on-year views come from one request per term covering the last 120 days plus the same weekdays one and two years earlier, so the years share a scale. Weeks run Monday–Sunday. The latest partial day is excluded.
                • 
      
              • Five-year and brand-comparison views are weekly (weeks start Sunday). Brands are joined onto one scale through an anchor brand that sits in every brand request (bet365 for Sportsbook, Sky Vegas for Gaming). Share of brand search is each brand's part of the total across the tracked brands, not market share.
                • 
      
              • Insufficient signal means the term is too sparse to compare: a weekly average below 3 on its scale, or two or more zero days (Google returns zeros for low-volume days). Those rows are left out of averages and charts.
                • 
      
              • Forecast is seasonal naive with a year-on-year trend factor (×0.5–×2 cap). It knows the calendar only through history.
                • 
      
              • Gaming: brand terms are the product names as typed ("sky vegas", "paddy power games"). Smaller products such as Betfair Bingo often have too little UK search volume for daily comparisons and are marked too sparse.
                • 
      
              • TV & entertainment: show names are broad searches (viewers, not only bettors). Social posts are marked by date; the change around a post is timing, not proof of cause.
                • 
      
              • Ambiguous terms: "coral", "boxing", "world cup", "starburst" and some show names include unrelated searches. Rising "not on GamStop" queries are unlicensed-operator demand, a compliance signal rather than a traffic target.
                • 
      
              • Refresh: a daily GitHub Action re-pulls the daily and year-on-year data and, about once a week, the five-year data. Failed pulls keep the last good copy and are flagged as stale above.
